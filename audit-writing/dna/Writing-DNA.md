# Writing DNA

> Operational rules for a mixed-author corpus of 20 accounting and auditing papers. These papers do not share one author. Do not sign a new piece with any of their names, and do not write as if a single person produced the set. Copy the moves. Do not copy the findings, the stimuli, or the bylines.
>
> Before drafting, read this file, `language-dna.md`, `structure-patterns.md`, `cognitive-framework.md`, `visual-style-guide.md`, and five `raw/` papers of the same type. Venue rules override citation punctuation. The user's assignment overrides scope. These rules override a generic "academic" template.

## Language Characteristics

Write in the first person plural. "We investigate," "we predict," "we find," "we also." Use "I" only in a genuinely single-authored paper; one paper in this corpus does, and it is the 1995 memory paper. Do not mix "I" and "we."

Sentences center on 22 words. Most fall between 18 and 33. One sentence in a paragraph may run to about 45 words if it holds a single comparison ("more X when A than when B, and this difference is larger when C"). A sentence under 12 words is allowed after a long one, to name a design fact or to hand off ("Hence, we predict that:"). Do not stack short sentences. Do not write three long sentences in a row.

Paragraphs are four to eight sentences, about 160 words. The hypothesis is its own paragraph. So is each numbered limitation.

The result verb is "find" or "show." The theory verb is "suggest" or "predict." "Associated with" belongs to archival results. "May" and "likely" hedge a mechanism you did not test. They do not hedge a result you did test.

"Also" is the ordinary additive. "However" and "In contrast" open the sentence that breaks the pattern. "Specifically" narrows. "Respectively" pairs numbers with conditions already named, in the same order. "Consistent with" requires a named theory or paper. "Prior research indicates that" is the setup, and the next sentence bounds it.

Repeat the dependent variable's name. Do not replace "investment willingness" or "the book-or-waive decision" with "this outcome" or "the above."

Parentheses hold citations, abbreviations, scale endpoints, and statistics. An em dash, used once or twice in a section, holds an appositive. Semicolons join two clauses or separate a complex list. Colons introduce a hypothesis, a "where" block, or a variable list. No exclamation point in the body. No rhetorical question. A labeled RQ is allowed only when the theory does not sign a direction. Quotation marks are for scale anchors and for a word used as a word.

Pick one citation convention and keep it. This corpus uses bracketed years, parenthetical years, and ampersands in different decades. Do not blend them. Put the citation immediately after the claim. Two or three citations in a sentence is normal. A theory paragraph with none is not.

Bold only the hypothesis label, an RQ label, a table title, and a manipulated word inside a stimulus. Headings are numbered and one to six words. No bullets in the argument. "First," "Second," inside the limitation paragraphs. No colored words. No humor, no metaphor you invented, no "importantly," no "very."

Older papers say "subjects." Later papers say "participants." Choose one.

## Structure Patterns

Match the type. Do not borrow the review's taxonomy for an experiment, or the experiment's manipulation checks for an archive.

**Experiment** (the default). Title is "Effects of X on Y," "The Joint Effect of X and Y on Z," or a question whose answer is the interaction. Abstract, 120–220 words: design, interaction, who should care. Introduction: the rule or the conflict, then the prior result, then "In this paper, we...," then the contribution as a boundary on one or two named streams, then a two-sentence roadmap. Do not open with "In this paper." Hypothesis section: one block per construct, label, then the hypothesis in words with no p-value. Method: participants (who, experience, exclusions), design, manipulations with one example sentence, measures, procedure. Results: manipulation checks, then hypotheses in order, means with standard deviations, the test, the tail. Simple effects after an interaction. A predicted null is reported in full. Conclusion: the condition restated, an implication for a named decision, numbered limitations that each name the next study. A second experiment is allowed when it turns a remedy on or off. Say only what changed.

**Archival.** Open with the mixed evidence and the missing moderator. One interaction hypothesis. List the proxies and the error in each. Sample-selection table. Equation, then "where," in variable order. Descriptives, then the coefficient in words, then alternative cutoffs as sentences, not as the word "robust." Interviews, if any, only to justify the proxy. Close on the proxy's limit.

**Review.** One sentence on what the review covers. Name the prior reviews and the difference in the buckets. Three buckets that are features of the setting. Each bucket ends on a gap. The close ties the gaps to a change in practice or standards. "We believe" is allowed only when the next clause names a feature of the setting.

Shared handoffs, and no others: "H2 predicts that..."; "The following limitations should be noted"; "Given this result, Experiment 2 tests whether..."

## Topic Selection Rules

Start from a conflict a design can split. Either a rule treats two mechanisms as optional, complementary, or forbidden, or prior papers disagree and the disagreement can be restated as a missing moderator, or a press claim and a published result point in opposite directions. The paper arrives after that conflict exists. It does not announce a new phenomenon.

The angle is the decision maker's processing: auditor, investor, manager, analyst. The sentence the paper can be quoted on names the condition. A main effect is the setup.

Do not write a how-to, a firm profile, a scandal narrative, or a character judgment. If there is a remedy, it is a change in the task (order, salience, a separate column, an instruction to generate an alternative), and it is tested, not urged.

The absence of tax and governmental topics in this set of 20 is a property of the sample. Do not treat it as a ban beyond the sample. Do treat "no condition, no paper" as a ban inside it.

## Source Strategy

Derive the prediction from one named psychological mechanism. Cite it where the prediction is made. Use a standard or a regulator only to justify why the question exists. The result comes from this study's experiment, archive, or field evaluations, with the comparison visible.

For a construct you cannot see cleanly (audit quality, independence, expertise), use more than one proxy and say what error each proxy has. For a person, recruit the person who makes the decision, and report experience. If the pool is students, say what kind and bound it in the limitations.

Declare the tail. One-tailed only for a directional prediction, marked, and said once. Give the p-value as a number. A sensitivity test names the alternative specification and what happened to the coefficient.

A predicted-pattern figure may shade a region. A results figure uses points and does not contain a number the table lacks. A mediation diagram carries the coefficient, the tail, and the interval. The prose states the same path. Cell means live in the paragraph as well as in the table. Logos, seals, and repository banners are not part of the style.

Do not import a finding, a mean, a case fact, or a stimulus sentence from these 20 papers into a new one.

## Core Cognitive Frames

1. Write "when," not "in general." The contribution is the feature under which a known effect appears, disappears, or reverses.
2. Require a match among person, task, and setting. Knowledge, accountability, mindset, and specialization do their work against a demand. They are not main-effect virtues.
3. Hold the economics constant and let attention move the judgment. The lever is presentation, order, or instruction. Test the lever. Do not preach it.
4. Do not mirror positive and negative cells unless the theory signed both. Asymmetry is often the result.
5. Prefer a disclosed noisy proxy, repeated, over one proxy treated as the construct.
6. Psychological mechanism in the hypothesis section. Institutional reader in the close: a standard setter, a regulator, a firm. Do not recommend a rule the design did not test.

If the theory is ordinal, predict a pattern and test a contrast. Do not claim every cell differs. Use "configural" or "substitutes" only when two institutional mechanisms replace rather than add to each other.

## Visual Style

One column for a manuscript. Serif, black on white, numbered short headings, footnotes for asides and for the tail convention. No cover image, no pull quote, no shaded finding box, no bullets.

Tables do the work: Panel A means (standard deviation) and n, Panel B the test, Panel C the contrasts. The note defines the coding and the stars. Figures, when the interaction is the point: categorical x-axis, one line per level of the second factor, solid and dashed so it survives grayscale, hue only as a second encoding, the condition's real name in the legend. Prediction figure and results figure use the same axes. A blue shade means "the theory allows this region" and appears only on the prediction. Put "[Insert Table 1 and Figure 1 about here]" in a working paper. The sentence before the exhibit names the comparison. The sentence after it can stand alone.

Appendices hold the instrument and the variable list, cited in order, and are not required for the reader to know the result.

## Failure modes

- A main effect with no condition, or a symmetric story the theory did not sign.
- "We find that X is important." Name the comparison and the number.
- A mechanism with no citation, or a citation with no claim.
- Stacked short sentences, a rhetorical question, a bold sentence in the argument, an exclamation point.
- A figure that is the only place the mean exists, or a predicted line drawn as if it were a result.
- Limitations that apologize instead of naming the next design.
- Any sentence that could only be true of one of these 20 papers.
